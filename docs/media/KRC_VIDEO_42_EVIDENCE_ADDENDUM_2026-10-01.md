## #6 Onion browning — partially supported
The National Onion Association identifies both sugar caramelization and Maillard reactions as contributors to browned onions, and states that exact cooking time depends on water content, cut, and volume. A primary food-engineering experiment measured onion non-enzymatic browning kinetics as a function of temperature and water activity. The video's universal '40 minutes, not 10' and 'only sugar breakdown' are overstated.
Sources:
- https://www.onions-usa.org/onionista/faster-caramelized-onions-with-baking-soda/
- https://www.onions-usa.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/NOA_Winter_Supplement_2019_Final.pdf
- https://doi.org/10.1016/0260-8774(95)90054-F

## #7 Washing mushrooms — experiment traced, not generalizable to all mushrooms
Harold McGee's experiment (described in independent reporting of *The Curious Cook*) reports 252 g of mushrooms increased to 258 g after five-minute immersion and blotting. This supports the numerical anecdote for its tested mushrooms, not an all-species absorption limit. USDA recommends washing fresh produce with running water before preparing it. Avoid soaking or leaving mushrooms wet unnecessarily.
Sources:
- https://www.scmp.com/article/413738/ask-chef
- https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/wordofmouth/2010/jan/18/should-you-wash-mushrooms
- https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-should-fresh-produce-be-washed-before-eating

## #8 Spinach oxalates — core supported; numeric range context required
Chai and Liebman (2005) measured boiling reductions in soluble oxalates of 30–87% across tested vegetables, compared with 5–53% for steaming; soluble oxalate lost from the food was recovered from cooking water. These are ranges across multiple vegetables, not guaranteed spinach-only reductions. A separate spinach/amaranth processing study reports lower oxalic acid and improved in-vitro iron availability after blanching/cooking. Kidney-stone-specific dietary advice must be individualized; this study does not establish prevention of stones in humans.
Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15826055/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12102364/

## #9 Beetroot roasting versus boiling — video's 'nothing is lost' contradicted
Ravichandran et al. (2013) compared roasting and boiling and found betalain declines under both treatments; antioxidant-capacity assays behaved differently and do not demonstrate superior clinical outcomes. Sawicki and Wiczkowski (2018) measured a 51–61% betalain decrease after boiling under their experimental conditions, moderated when peel was present. Neither supports universal superiority of roasting or zero losses.
Sources:
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096399691100442X
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29680057/

## #28 Honey in boiling water — partial mechanism, no universal health verdict
Reviews document thermal sensitivity of honey components and HMF generation during heating and storage. This does not establish that a spoonful of honey in hot tea is toxic, devoid of all nutritional properties, or that a single temperature threshold determines clinical effects.
Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29619623/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34626076/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26593496/
