# KRC — numeric/source audit, phase 3 (2026-10-01)

Original: docs/media/KRC_VIDEO_TRANSCRIPT_42_FRESH_2026-10-01.md. All claims below refer to the video's own wording; external evidence is explicitly separated.

## #12 Rice / arsenic — supported with important trade-off
Video: rinse according to texture; excess-water cooking more effective for arsenic than rinsing. FDA research: rinsing has minimal arsenic effect; cooking with 6–10 parts water per part rice and draining reduces inorganic arsenic 40–60%, but loses 50–70% of some added folate, iron, niacin and thiamine in enriched rice. The video omits the nutrient trade-off.
FDA: https://www.fda.gov/food/environmental-contaminants-food/what-you-can-do-limit-exposure-arsenic
Primary study: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26515534/

## #14 Pearl barley — partly supported, unsupported universal phytate claim
Soaking affects barley texture and cooking properties in tested barley products. A malting study shows that phytate degradation can occur under specific steeping/germination conditions, especially lactic acid/high-temperature treatment. This does NOT prove that simply soaking household pearl barley overnight reliably lowers phytate or improves iron uptake in humans. Its beta-glucan content is established, but not a universal fixed glycemic index.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30372993/ ; https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.1724

## #19–21 Seafood and liver — universal minutes and visual cues unsafe
Video says shrimp 1.5–3 minutes, squid 1 or 40 minutes, liver 1.5–2 minutes per side with a slightly pink center. These are culinary heuristics, not validated universal safe times. USDA safe chart lists fish/shellfish 145°F (63°C), all poultry giblets 165°F (74°C). USDA explicitly warns chicken liver may harbor Campylobacter and Salmonella internally and should reach 165°F throughout. USDA beef organ meats 160°F (71°C). Video's universal pink-center liver instruction is not suitable food safety guidance.
Sources: https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart ; https://www.fsis.usda.gov/ChickenLiver ; https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/meat-catfish/beef-farm-table

## #30 Coffee — cited SCA range applies to traditional espresso definition
Video says SCA 93±2°C and below 87°C inevitably sour; classic SCAA espresso definition uses 90.5–96.1°C (195–205°F), not a universal mandate for all brewing techniques or roast levels. Cooling boiled water for exactly 30–40 seconds does not guarantee a fixed temperature across kettles/volumes.
SCA: https://sca.coffee/sca-news/25-magazine/issue-3/defining-ever-changing-espresso-25-magazine-issue-3-zyx36

## #31 Green tea — categorical temperature/kinetics contradicted by experimental variation
Video recommends 70–80°C and claims amino acids below 80°C extract faster than catechins, and >85°C causes uniformly excessive catechin extraction. A 2026 comparative study of eight tea varieties found brewing at 100°C increased catechin extraction for most, while some varieties required 80–90°C to avoid degradation; extraction kinetics of free amino acids and catechins varied by tea. Earlier studies also found optimal catechin extraction can be 80°C for 30 min or different conditions depending on target molecule. Sensory preference for cooler brewing is not a universal biochemical threshold.
Sources: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002364382600263X ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21905216/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4573099/

## #34 Yeast — video range depends on yeast type
Video gives universal 35–40°C and says ~55°C kills yeast. Fleischmann's recommends 38–43°C for active dry yeast rehydration but 49–54°C liquid for its RapidRise/bread-machine products **when combined with dry ingredients**; manufacturer recipe matters. No universal lethal threshold can be inferred from a single temperature without duration and medium.
Source: https://www.fleischmannsyeast.com/baking-basics/yeast-101
